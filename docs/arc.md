Season 2 opens on Day 37 with the flood between Cal and the new voice, and the question of whether Cal's own message was a rescue or a trap.

## The truth behind the story

The writers know five things the audience learns slowly or never, and every episode has to agree with them.

1. The message is one dead man's hope. The operator at the repeater hut was a volunteer emergency officer holding a pre-war evacuation plan that set ten as the smallest group a helicopter would lift. He recorded the message on Day 1, died around Day 5, and a solar battery kept the loop running. No Northern Recovery Authority ever answered him.
2. The plane is real and useless. Someone far south is surveying the living from the air and will not land in fallout. The flights map groups; they do not collect them.
3. The vehicles are the collectors. A convoy working out of the mine south of Pine Creek follows the survey pattern and takes groups for labour and for their supplies. Hollis finds and gathers tens for them. "Nine, need one" is his pitch, and the people he gathers are his payment.
4. The rule is right for the wrong reason. The operator wrote "do not travel after dark" because night driving on that highway kills people. It stays true because Hollis's people move at night.
5. Nobody is coming. Season 1 never contradicts this, and Season 2 can only complicate it.

Two craft rules follow. The narration reports only what Cal saw or was told, so the visuals carry everything Cal missed, and the gap between the two is where the suspense lives. And no episode explains a thing before the story needs it: the black rain is a wall of dots for nineteen episodes before Priya says what it does.
