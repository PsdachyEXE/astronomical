# TALLY — Series Plan, Episode 1 Script and Build Brief

Oct 18, 2026 · @Lachie

## Premise and format

TALLY is a serialised, narrator-only animated short. After a nuclear exchange across the Top End, a looped radio message promises rescue to groups of ten, and a lone road train driver on the Stuart Highway starts counting. Episodes run 70 to 75 seconds, one a day, and every one opens the same way: a card reading DAY n above a row of tally strokes, and the narrator saying "Day n. Tally: n."

The idea earns its place three ways. The setting is new: the outback is already a horizon line, a sun and a road, so the minimal style is the landscape rather than a compromise. The engine is a number: viewers come back to see it move, and it only has to move by one to be an episode. The antagonist is a person, not a monster, which keeps the animation cheap and the dread real.

| Decision | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runtime | 70 to 75 s per episode; floor 62 s, ceiling 170 s | Over the one-minute line that TikTok's and Snapchat's programmes require; under the three-minute cap on Shorts, Reels and Spotlight; long enough for a climb and a climax, short enough to bank one a day |
| Frame | 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264 MP4, AAC audio | One master for every platform; per-platform variants differ only in layout margins and encode settings |
| Cadence | One episode per posting day; story days are not consecutive | A month of posts covers two months of story, and skipped days are how a log reads |
| Opening ritual | Card DAY n with tally strokes; narrator says "Day n. Tally: n." | A signature in the first second, and the number is the hook |
| Voice | Narrator only; other characters are reported, never performed | One TTS voice; the two radio voices are the only exception |
| Captions | Burned in by default, word-timed from the TTS timestamps; a per-platform toggle | Most viewers watch muted, and the timing data already exists |
| Series name | TALLY (working title) | Short, searchable, names the device; swapping it changes nothing else |
| Episode engine | One climb, one climax, one sting; the sting carries a fact the viewer did not have | Each video stands alone and pulls to the next |
| Hidden detail | One thing in frame per episode the narrator never mentions | Rewatches and comments, and comments are reach |

Money comes from YouTube first and Facebook second; Instagram and TikTok are reach. The runtime stays at 70 seconds so every episode is eligible everywhere the day a programme opens.

Platform ad revenue is small at this scale: a million Shorts views is tens of US dollars, not hundreds. The series earns properly once the audience can be pointed at something. YouTube memberships and Super Thanks open at 500 subscribers, and a game or product of your own, a brand deal or a long-form channel each pay far more per viewer than the Shorts pool. All of it is taxable income, so get an ABN before the first payout.

## The world

The whole world is three hundred kilometres of the Stuart Highway between two dead cities, in the last weeks of the dry.

Darwin to the north and the Tindal air base outside Katherine to the south were both hit. Nobody in the story knows by whom, and nobody ever finds out; the war is weather. The survivors live on the stretch between Adelaide River and Pine Creek: a two-lane highway, roadhouses, cattle stations, a war cemetery, termite mounds and a horizon that never gets closer. Darwin is a dark smudge at the top of the frame and Katherine a smudge at the bottom, and the story never goes into either.

It is October when the bombs fall. The build-up is the clock. The Wet arrives around Day 30 of the story, and when it does the rivers rise, the highway floods and the rain brings down what the sky has been holding. Season 1 ends with the station cut off by water.

The land does the art direction. A flat line, a disc of sun, one road, three shapes walking: that is a finished frame.

### The message

Verbatim, never varied, always through a radio filter:

> Northern Recovery Authority. Aircraft will not land for fewer than ten. Gather. Report your number on channel seven at last light. Do not travel after dark.

The Northern Recovery Authority is invented. The script never uses a real agency's name, the real emergency warning signal or real emergency-broadcast wording. A convincing fake emergency message is a problem in its own right, and the platforms treat it as one.

### Rules of the world

Each rule is stated once, in the episode where it first matters, and never explained before the story needs it.

| Rule | Where it comes from | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nobody travels after dark | The message | Anyone moving at night is either outside the message or ignoring it; Hollis's people move at night |
| The tally is people present at last light | Cal's own rule, Episode 4 | Leaving or dying removes a mark; a removed mark is struck through, never erased |
| Water is the currency | The trailer: roughly ten people for forty days | Cal never says the exact count; Marcus counts pallets instead of people |
| Nobody stands in black rain | Episode 3 | Priya explains what it does in Episode 20; until then it is a wall of dark dots that people run from |
| Report the number on channel seven at last light | The message | Nobody answers until Day 19; a reply turns out to be the most frightening sound in the series |

## Visual language

Everything on screen is a circle, a rectangle, a triangle or a line, in a short fixed palette, and the only face is a mouth.

### Canvas and safe zones

1080 × 1920 at 30 fps, one master for every platform. Scenery bleeds to the frame edge so the video looks native everywhere; anything that must be read sits inside a stage rectangle that clears every platform's overlays at once. Each platform's own zone lives in the build's platform config, so a per-platform render can use the extra room, and the numbers get re-measured against an unlisted test upload on a real phone whenever a platform changes its interface.

| YouTube Shorts | 120 px | 300 px (360 with the description expanded) | 0 | 96 px | [adaptlypost](https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide) |

The universal stage is x 72 to 948 and y 220 to 1540, which is 876 × 1320 px. The title card and the interstitial tally cards are centred at y 860, burned-in captions sit on one line at y 1440, and the hidden details and the handprint stay inside the stage. The horizon stays at y 1130. These are third-party measurements of interfaces that change without notice, so the build draws them as an overlay for checking and the launch is preceded by an unlisted upload on each platform.

### Figure construction (1.0 scale, wide shot)

- Head: circle, radius 36.
- Torso: rounded rectangle 72 × 110, corner radius 14, top edge 8 px below the head.
- Arms and legs: 8 px stroked lines with round caps; arms from the torso's top corners, legs from the bottom corners. No hands, no feet.
- Mouth: one 28 px stroke, 5 px wide, with a curve from −1 (frown) through 0 (flat) to +1 (smile). It is the only facial feature. There are no eyes, ever.
- Facing: the mouth sits in the left third of the head facing left, the right third facing right, centred facing camera, and is hidden facing away. Direction is read from where the mouth is.
- Scale: an adult stands about one eighth of frame height in a wide shot (about 240 px). Toby is 0.7. Per-character proportion tweaks never exceed ×1.3.

### Environment primitives

The cab is a 300 × 200 rectangle with an inset windscreen rectangle; each trailer is 520 × 170; wheels are circles of radius 28. Roofs and termite mounds are triangles. Buildings are rectangles with one darker door rectangle. Debris is 10 to 18 px squares; falling glass is 6 px squares. Rain is 3 px dots falling at 1,400 px per second with 2% jitter; black rain is the same in ink. Dust is a 60 px band of day-sky at 30% opacity sitting on the horizon. The road is a wedge from the bottom edge to a vanishing point on the horizon.

### Motion vocabulary

This is the complete list. Nothing else is animated.

| Move | What happens | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| idle | scale 1.00 to 1.02 and back | 3 s loop |
| walk | body bob ±6 px, limbs swing ±25° | 0.8 s per step |
| turn | mouth slides across the head | 6 frames |
| flinch | 8 px jump, 2-frame squash to 0.92 height | 10 frames |
| collapse | rotate 90°, drop to the ground line | 18 frames |
| still | nothing moves, idle off | as long as the dread needs |
| strike | a diagonal drawn through a tally mark | 12 frames |

### Camera and cuts

Locked wide shots. The only camera move is a slow push, scale 1.00 to 1.06 across a scene. Hard cuts only: no pans, no dissolves, no fades, except cut-to-white for the flash and cut-to-black for the end. Walking along the highway is faked: the figure stays put, the road dashes scroll, the horizon never moves.

### Cards

DAY n in a monospace display face, centred. The tally beneath it is drawn as strokes in five-bar gates, each stroke drawn on over 6 frames, never typed characters. A struck mark keeps its place with a diagonal through it. There is no persistent HUD; a change in the tally during an episode gets a 1.2-second card at the moment it happens.

### Captions

Most viewers watch muted, so the narration is burned in as captions by default: one line, the monospace face at 44 px in text colour, centred at y 1440, timed from the TTS word timestamps, with the radio's lines in the same face at 60% opacity. The caption line never holds more than two rows. The toggle is per platform in the build's config, and turning it off changes nothing else.

### Off limits

Nothing from Fallout: no vault suits, wrist computers, power armour, branded drinks, retro-futurism or mascots. The reference is the mood, not the furniture. No real brand logos on the roadhouse or the truck.

## Audio language

One narrator, two filtered radio voices, sound only when the plot makes it, and silence everywhere else.

- Narrator: one TTS voice, low and dry, Australian if the provider has one. Pace around 130 words a minute. The script marks pauses, and the pauses are the performance; a line that lands early loses its weight. Check that the provider's licence allows commercial use of the generated audio before Episode 1 goes up. Recording it yourself is the fallback and a differentiator.
- Radio voices: a second TTS voice for the Authority loop (flat, clipped, about 165 words a minute) and a third for Hollis. Both band-passed 300 to 3400 Hz with a static bed underneath and a click at the start and end of every transmission.
- Sound effects: plot only. Footsteps on gravel, doors, glass, engines, the radio, rain, bangs. No ambient loops except wind. No birds, no insects, no traffic. The silence is a character, and a viewer who notices there are no birds has understood the world.
- Music: none in daylight. A single low drone, about 50 Hz and barely audible, under night scenes only; it cuts dead on the final line of an episode.
- Loudness: narration at −16 LUFS integrated, true peak −1.5 dBTP. Effects sit under the narration, except the flash, the boom and the bangs, which are allowed to peak above it.
- Sources: CC0 libraries only (Freesound with the CC0 filter, or similar), kept in a manifest with the URL and licence beside each file.

## Episode shape

Every episode is the same 70 to 75 seconds in five parts, and the sting is the part that matters most.

| Part | Time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card | 0 to 3 s | DAY n, tally strokes drawn on, the narrator's signature line |
| Situation | 3 to 20 s | Where Cal is and what changed since the last day; one concrete image |
| Escalation | 20 to 45 s | The problem grows; the hidden detail appears here and is never mentioned |
| Climax | 45 to 62 s | The one event the episode exists for; sound carries it and narration thins out |
| Sting | 62 to 72 s | One or two lines containing a fact the viewer did not have, then cut to black and a 1.5-second end card showing the tally |

Four rules hold every episode to the shape. The first spoken line after the signature is never a sentence the viewer could predict. Narration stays under 100 words, and the radio adds at most 30. The climax has at least two seconds with no voice at all. The sting is never a question. Shorts, Reels and Spotlight all loop, so the end card cuts to black and the next frame of the loop is the DAY card: no fade, no logo, a clean seam.
